For each book we say how far the check has got. «Verified»: the first publication (book, magazine or registration) is documented by two independent sources or by a record in the US copyright register, and no source points to an earlier publication. «Edition confirmed»: a source other than Wikidata confirms the year of the edition, but the first publication is not verified yet. «To be confirmed»: all other cases. What it really means

Before you publish
01

The text, not the edition

The original text is free. Recent translations, illustrations and edited editions have their own rights: to publish a translation you need a new one, or one that is already free.

02

It depends where you publish

On 1 January not everything expires everywhere at once. The same book can be free in the USA and protected in Europe, or the other way round. «Strong Poison» has been free in the USA since 2026, and in Italy, France, Germany and the UK only from 2028.

03

Certain and to be checked

We mark as certain only what more than one source confirms (years of death, years of publication). The rest is «to be checked», with the reason. Sources are listed at the bottom of every page.

The short answers

Questions

It is 1 January of every year, the day copyright expires for works that have reached the legal limit: in the European Union and the UK 70 years after the author's death (Directive 2006/116/EC, Art. 1), in the USA 95 years after publication (17 U.S.C. § 304).

In Italy, France, Germany and the UK, the works of writers who died in 1957. In Spain, those of writers who died in 1947. In the USA, books published in 1932.

For authors who died before 7 December 1987, Spanish law still applies the 80 years of the 1879 law (Ley de Propiedad Intelectual, fourth transitional provision).
