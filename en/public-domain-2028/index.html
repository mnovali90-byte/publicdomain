Questions

Public domain, in plain words

What enters the public domain in 2028?

It depends on the country. In the USA, works published in 1932 (95 years after publication, 17 U.S.C. § 304). In the European Union, the works of authors who died in 1957 (author's life + 70 years, Directive 2006/116/EC, Art. 1).

Why are the USA and Europe different?

They use different rules. The USA counts from the year a work was published. Europe counts from the year the author died. So a book can be free in one place and still protected in the other.

Can I publish a book from 1932 in Europe or the UK?

Not automatically. In Europe what matters is when the author died: a book from 1932 by Virginia Woolf (died 1941) is already free, while one by Dashiell Hammett (died 1961) is protected until 2031 under the 70-year rule. The EU directive lets protection end earlier for non-EU works (Art. 7), but countries apply it differently: in Italy the Court of Cassation applied 70 years even to a US author. The UK applies the shorter term of the country of origin (section 12(6)), unless the book was also published in the UK at the same time, as many American novels were. Some countries have their own rules (Spain gives 80 years to authors who died before 1987): check case by case.

Is every work published in 1932 really free in the USA?

Not always. Works first published in the USA whose copyright was not renewed after 28 years were already free earlier. Foreign works that regained protection in 1996 (17 U.S.C. § 104A) last 95 years from publication, but only if they were still protected in their country of origin and the author was from a member country. Our list shows works that probably become free; it is not a legal clearance.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is general information built from open data, and it may contain errors. Before publishing, check the law and the facts for your country and your edition.
